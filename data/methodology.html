Aim

To document, compare and visualise translation challenges in selected English, Russian and Kazakh aviation texts, linking every analytical observation to its source passage and supporting criterion-based evaluation of translation choices.

Research questions

  1. Which translation difficulties recur when aviation terminology and phraseology are rendered between English, Russian and Kazakh?
  2. Which strategies are used to resolve these difficulties, and how consistently?
  3. How do the chosen strategies affect terminological accuracy, clarity, preservation of meaning and functional equivalence?

Source selection criteria

  • Authoritative aviation documents: ICAO and FAA materials and official sources of the Republic of Kazakhstan.
  • Availability of at least two language versions, or of comparable texts on the same subject.
  • Permission to quote the material in a public research prototype.
  • Planned scope: about 15–20 document sets, subject to the availability of comparable materials.

Alignment and coding procedure

  1. Each document set is segmented into units of analysis; one unit may link one sentence to several (merging or splitting).
  2. The link type is recorded: confirmed translation, parallel version with unknown direction, or comparable text. Direction is recorded only when it is documented.
  3. Doubtful alignments are marked In review.
  4. Selected spans are coded with the difficulty categories and strategies listed below, with an explanation and a supporting source.
  5. Every equivalent and definition is checked against relevant specialist sources; unchecked forms are marked Pending validation.

Limitations of the sample

The corpus is small and purposive: frequencies describe the sample and cannot be generalised to aviation discourse as a whole. Translation direction is often undocumented in public materials. Visitor assessments in this prototype are illustrative and are not part of the research data.

Stages of work

  1. Selection and collection of source documents.
  2. Alignment of EN, RU and KK fragments.
  3. Annotation of difficulties and strategies.
  4. Validation of equivalents against specialist sources.
  5. Rubric-based evaluation and statistical summary.
  6. Interpretation of the results for the dissertation.
