Master's dissertation “Translation Challenges in Aviation Discourse” · «Особенности перевода авиационного дискурса» · «Авиациялық дискурсты аудару ерекшеліктері»
Master's student: Махмут Лайла Женисқызы · Supervisor: Акижанова Диана Муратовна

Aim

To document, compare and visualise translation challenges in selected English, Russian and Kazakh aviation texts, linking every analytical observation to its source passage and supporting criterion-based evaluation of translation choices.

Research questions

  1. Which translation difficulties recur when aviation terminology and phraseology are rendered between English, Russian and Kazakh?
  2. Which strategies are used to resolve these difficulties, and how consistently across documents?
  3. How do the chosen strategies affect terminological accuracy, clarity, preservation of meaning and functional equivalence?

Source selection

  • ICAO documents: Doc 4444 (PANS-ATM, Chapter 12 Phraseologies), Doc 9432 (Manual of Radiotelephony), Annexes 1, 10, 13 and 14, Doc 8400, Doc 9835, including the official ICAO Russian versions where they exist.
  • FAA documents: Pilot/Controller Glossary, Aeronautical Information Manual, Order JO 7110.65.
  • Official sources of the Republic of Kazakhstan: the Rules of phraseology and radiotelephony (reg. No. 6635, as amended in 2023) and Law No. 339-IV on the use of airspace and aviation activities; the Air Code of the Russian Federation for comparison.
  • Research monographs on aviation communication (Cushing 1994; Estival, Farris and Molesworth 2016).
  • Version 0.2 contains 21 sources (19 documents and two records for the researcher's own translations and training texts), 54 term entries and 26 units of analysis, grouped into 11 topics shared by terms and units; the plan foresees 15–20 document sets, depending on the availability of comparable materials.

Alignment and coding procedure

  1. Each unit of analysis links fragments in EN, RU and KK with their sources and locators.
  2. The link type is recorded explicitly for each language, as the relation of its text to the original: confirmed translation (direction documented by the source, e.g. an official ICAO translation), parallel version with unknown direction (authentic texts in several languages, e.g. laws of Kazakhstan in Russian and Kazakh), comparable texts (same genre and function, not translations of each other, e.g. ICAO and national phraseology) or illustrative translation (made by the researcher for demonstration; not a source).
  3. Doubtful alignments are marked In review.
  4. Selected spans are coded with difficulty categories and strategies from the working lists below, with an explanation.
  5. Every form and annotation carries a validation status:
    • Source referenced — wording follows the known model of an ICAO/FAA document and has an exact reference; it must be checked verbatim against the official edition before the defence.
    • Pending validation — the equivalent must be checked against a specialist source, especially Kazakh forms and Russian/Kazakhstani phraseology.
    • Illustrative example — the researcher's own translation or a modelled text (the call sign ASTRA 451, the METAR and ATIS texts are fictional).
    • Verified — set by the researcher after checking; no record has this status in version 0.2.

Limitations of the sample

  • The corpus is small and purposive: frequencies describe the sample and cannot be generalised to aviation discourse as a whole.
  • During preparation the ICAO, FAA and adilet.zan.kz websites were not accessible, so quoted wording and paragraph numbers still have to be checked against the official editions.
  • In Kazakhstan radiotelephony is conducted in English and Russian. The Kazakh radiotelephony phrases in the corpus are therefore translation models, not a current norm — which is itself an argument for research on the terminological normalisation of Kazakh aviation language.
  • Records marked as demo (modelled METAR, ATIS, cabin announcement and manual instruction) are excluded from the research statistics.
  • Visitor assessments made in this prototype are illustrative and are not part of the research data.

Language and aviation accidents

Dates and figures follow the published official investigations; verify them against the primary sources before citing.

  • Tenerife, 27 March 1977 (KLM 4805 / Pan Am 1736): collision of two Boeing 747s on the runway at Los Rodeos, 583 fatalities. Non-standard phraseology was among the factors: the KLM crew's “at take-off” was understood ambiguously. ICAO afterwards reserved the word take-off for issuing or cancelling a take-off clearance and departure for all other cases (see unit U014: the Russian «к взлёту готов» does not reflect this rule).
  • Avianca 052, 25 January 1990, New York: a Boeing 707 crashed after fuel exhaustion, 73 fatalities. The crew reported low fuel but did not use the standard word emergency or the MAYDAY signal, and the urgency was not recognised — the proposition was kept, the illocutionary force was lost (difficulty “pragmatic function”).
  • Charkhi Dadri, 12 November 1996: mid-air collision of a Saudi Arabian Airlines Boeing 747 (flight 763) and a Kazakhstan Airlines Il-76 (flight 1907), 349 fatalities. The investigation pointed to the Kazakh crew descending below the assigned level and to insufficient proficiency in radiotelephony English. The accident was one of the arguments for the ICAO language proficiency requirements (Annex 1, applicable from 5 March 2008).

Rubric example

Unit U003 (conditional clearance), EN → RU, translation «АСТРА 451, после посадки Аэробуса 320 на прямой занимайте исполнительный старт за ним». Training example; it matches the acceptance case of the specification.

  • Terminological accuracy — 2: line up → «занимайте исполнительный старт», an established equivalent.
  • Clarity — 1: «за ним» replaces the repeated aircraft type; the meaning is recoverable from context, but redundancy is reduced.
  • Preservation of meaning — 2: condition, object and action are preserved.
  • Functional equivalence — 2: the speech act “conditional clearance” is preserved; the condition precedes the instruction.
  • Total: 7 / 8.

Stages of work and validation plan

  1. Collect the official ICAO texts (Doc 4444, Doc 9432, Annexes 1, 10, 13, 14) and check units U015–U018 and U025–U026 verbatim, adding exact paragraph numbers.
  2. Check the FAA Pilot/Controller Glossary definitions: clearance, runway, roger, wilco, squawk, line up and wait, NOTAM.
  3. Compare Law No. 339-IV (Article 1) in Russian and Kazakh and replace illustrative Kazakh forms with authentic ones where they exist.
  4. Check the Russian phraseology against the Rules of phraseology and radiotelephony (No. 6635) and complete the locators.
  5. Discuss the Kazakh phraseology with experts (air traffic controllers, instructors of the Academy of Civil Aviation) — a possible empirical part of the work (interviews or a questionnaire).
  6. Set the status to verified after checking, rebuild the corpus and interpret the results for the dissertation.
